\GRPchapter{Gameplay}

Gameplay in \GammaRP{} is divided into \emph{open} mode and \emph{phased} mode.

\section{Open mode}

Unless the narrative of the game requires extended sequences of rapid action, it's best to keep the game in \emph{open} mode.

Open mode consists of the GM describing a general setting, and the players asking questions to build on the setting and decide what to do. Whenever there's a questionable chance of success to something the players would like to do, the GM will ask them to make relevant rolls.

This is best illustrated with an example.

\begin{GRPquicknotewide}
\GRPsay{GM}{As you enter the ruins, you notice the ground change beneath you, from soft dirt to sharp gravel. The sounds of wildlife grow eerily quiet.}
\GRPsay{}{Pillars of stone are arranged in a path to the temple, some of them are cracked and fallen. You see the mysterious door described by the pirates, but there's a pillar that has fallen over and is blocking it.}
\GRPsay{Alice}{Huh. Can we see past the broken pillar?}
\GRPsay{GM}{You can! The top half of the door is visible, but you can't see the inset for the key you found earlier.}
\GRPsay{Eve}{I could move the pillar out of the way.}
\GRPsay{Bob}{I could also try to worm my way past the pillar to reach the keyhole.}
\GRPsay{Eve}{Oh! good point, maybe try that first.}
\GRPsay{Bob}{I take the key and try to squeeze my way past the pillar.}
\GRPsay{GM}{This requires grace of body. It's an extremely odd angle, so roll with \GRPadditionald{2}}
\GRPrheader{Athletics Roll}
\GRPsay{Bob}{\GRProll{2}{8}\GRPmodroll{}{-2} $\Rightarrow$ \\
				\GRProll{2}{6} - \GRPdicelist[4]{3,5}}
\GRPrfooter{}
\GRPsay{Bob}{Awww, so close.}
\GRPsay{GM}{You try several times, Bob, but you just can't squeeze in the key.}
\vspace{4\baselineskip}
\GRPsay{Eve}{Let me move it for you, Bob. \\ Could I lift the pillar up?}
\GRPsay{GM}{Okay- That will require strength of body, and the pillar is incredibly heavy. I'd say it's nearly impossible to perform this task, and it has a risk of injury to body on failure. It would need a roll for \GRPtalent{Fortitude} with \GRPadditionald{8}.}
\GRPsay{Alice}{I'd like to look for a broken piece of stone or something we could use as a wedge to make that easier. Doesn't seem right to risk getting injured before even starting this dungeon.}
\GRPsay{GM}{Sure! That seems reasonable. Seems like Grace of Mind to find a clever way to use your surroundings to make this task easier. Roll me \GRPtalent{Insight}.}
\GRPrheader{Insight Roll}
\GRPsay{Alice}{\GRProll{3}{5} - \GRPdicelist[5]{5,5,9}}
\GRPrfooter{}
\GRPsay{GM}{Great. Alice you notice that one side of the pillar is resting on the stone base on top of a shard of rock. A strong enough person should be able to knock out that rock and let the pillar roll aside. I'll say this brings down the difficulty of the task to hard- requiring \GRPadditionald{4}. You still risk injury on failure.}
\GRPsay{Alice}{I suggest pushing out the rock to Eve.}
\GRPsay{Eve}{I'll take Alice's suggestion.}
\GRPsay{Bob}{Wait! We should all be able to do this together. I can help Eve push that rock.}
\GRPsay{GM}{Sure! Eve give me a \GRPtalent{Fortitude} roll, and Bob give me a helping roll.}
\GRPrheader{Fortitude Roll}
\GRPsay{Eve}{\GRProll{6}{4}\GRPmodroll{}{-4} $\Rightarrow$ \\
				\GRProll{6}{0} - \GRPdicelist[0]{11,4,6,9,4,2}}
\GRPsay{Bob}{\GRProll{2}{3}\GRPmodroll{}{-4} $\Rightarrow$ \\
				\GRProll{2}{-1} - \GRPdicelist[1]{2,12}}
\GRPrfooter{}
\GRPsay{GM}{Wow! You both heave at the same time and manage to roll the pillar onto it's side so that there's more space near the door. Bob your earlier frustration with the pillar really comes out here and you help Eve bring it over the edge.}
\end{GRPquicknotewide}

Note the back and forth between the GM and the players in this example. The GM poses a problem to the players- namely that there is a pillar blocking the keyhole to the door. The players propose various solutions to the problem, and can even add to the environment by asking the GM about things that could plausibly exist.

As a GM, when a player suggests something, it behooves you to find something similar that could possibly work to keep the game going.

\subsection{Taking Damage}

As the players explore the environment, dangers such as traps or hostile creatures or characters may deal damage to them. The number of wounds a player sustains is equal to the damage dealt. 

Damage can be either elemental, physical, or direct.

For elemental and physical damage, armor reduces the amount of damage that a player sustains. Plain armor without resistance reduces amount of damage by the weight of the armor. 

Armor can have elemental or physical resistance. Elemental resistance armor reduces elemental damage by an additional 1. Physical resistance armor reduces physical damage by an additional 1.

Armor or other equipment may grant a parry which corresponds to one of the talents. When a character has a parry, this talent allows them to block damage using that talent. Roll the talent listed by the parry and incoming damage is reduced additionally by your number of successes on the parry roll.

Direct damage is not reduced by armor. A character taking direct damage will take a number of wounds equal to the damage.

Direct damage may also not be parried.

Injuries are also not reduced by armor, and may not be parried.

Incoming damage is also reduced by the amount of cover a character has, which is often only relevant in Phased mode.

\subsection{Poison and Disease}

Poison and disease come in three basic types in \GammaRP: Precision poison or disease, Strength poison or disease, and Grace poison or disease.

When poison or disease is applied to a character, it automatically depletes 1 from the corresponding characteristic.

\subsection{Downtime}
Downtime is a special part of \emph{open} gameplay where a character takes care of slower tasks after the action for the day calms down.

Downtime consists of the following for each character:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item Perform downtime actions - the GM must grant a minimum of 2 downtime actions. They may grant an additional bonus downtime action as appropriate, and rarely may grant 2 bonus downtime actions.
	\begin{itemize}
		\item If one of your actions is \textbf{not} \GRPability{Recuperate}, deplete \textbf{one} Foundation of your choice (Mind, Body, Heart) \textbf{in addition} to \textbf{not} resetting your depleted fundamentals.
	\end{itemize}
	\item Choose character's loadout for the next day.
\end{enumerate}

The possible downtime actions are listed below.

\begin{GRPquicknotewide}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \GRPability{Study}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Memory}. Increment your study counter by the number of successes you get. When your study counter hits 12 - you reset it to 0 and create a new wt 0 cast focus of your choice (not automatically prepped).
	\item \GRPability{Scavenge}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Intellect}. Gain scavenged loot of value equal to 100 gold times the number of successes you get. Ask your GM what loot you obtain. 
	\item \GRPability{Tinker}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Insight}. Increment your tinker counter by the number of successes you get. When your tinker counter hits 12 you may either
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Create a new item of craft grade 1
		\item Create an upgrade that would increase the craft grade of another item by 1
		\item Increase the amount of grade of a previously created upgrade by 1.
	\end{itemize}
	Upgrades may be applied immediately or at any point during freeform gameplay. You do not need to finalize the properties of the upgrade until your final application of the upgrade.
	\item \GRPability{Practice}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Dexterity}. Increment your practice counter by the number of successes you get. When your practice counter hits 12, reset it to 0, and you may increase the rank of a skill that has 1 rank to 2 ranks. This may be a skill on either your own character, or on any character that has been nearby who you could have been practicing with.
	\item \GRPability{Develop}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Fortitude}. Increment your Development counter by the number of successes you get. When your Development counter hits 12, reset it to 0 and grant yourself or any other player character close to you 1 XP.
	\item \GRPability{Train}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Athletics}. Increment your Training counter by the number of successes you get. When your Training counter hits 12, reset it to 0 and you may add 1 rank to a Skill that has no ranks in it. This may be a skill on either your own character, or on any character that has been nearby for significant periods of time who you ostensibly could have been practicing with.
	\item \GRPability{Explore}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Perception}. If successful, Roll from the exploration table to determine a new area that your character discovers. You may adjust your table roll by a number up to the number of successes you got on the exploration roll.
	\item \GRPability{Enhance}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Will}. Increment your Enhance counter by the number of successes you get. When your Augmentation counter hits 12, reset it to 0 and either
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Add a new augment to your character or any nearby willing character.
		\item Replace an augment on a character (yours or any nearby willing) with a different augment
		\item Replace two augments with an attunement on your own character or any nearby willing character.
		\item Replace an attunement with another attunement or two augments, on your own character or any nearby willing character. The affected character loses any XP invested in the original attunement.
	\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPability{Socialize}-- Roll \GRPtalent{Charisma}. For each success on your roll, you may either
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Obtain a piece of plot information from gossip or news.
		\item Start a rumour that will reach a specific NPC your character is aware of.
		\item Make an NPC who is negative towards your party neutral towards your party.
		\item Make an NPC who is neutral towards your party positive towards your party.
	\end{itemize}
	You may not adjust the attitude of an NPC who is explicitly hostile towards your party.
	\item \GRPability{Shop}-- Ask the GM if this action is available. Purchase equipmentent and sell equipment. By default, equipment sells for 50\% of the price of new equipment. You may roll \GRPtalent{Perception} to find bargains and decrease the price of buying a piece of new equipment by 10\% per success on your perception roll. You may roll \GRPtalent{Charisma} to sell your old equipment for higher prices and increase the price of selling a piece of equipment by 10\% per success on your charisma roll.
	\item \GRPability{Celebrate}-- Ask the GM if this action is available. This action requires two or more player characters to perform this same action during the same downtime. The next day, all participating player characters will 1 bonus rank to their archetype.
	\item \GRPability{Recuperate}-- Consumes the equivalent of 1 ration. Your character eats and sleeps (if applicable) and tends to their wounds. Perform the following:
		\begin{enumerate}
			\item Reset any depleted fundamentals.
			\item Clear all wounds
			\item Reset cast uses
			\item Reset loadout change uses
			\item Reset uses of an archetype
			\item Reset bonus ranks in an archetype.
    	\end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\end{GRPquicknotewide}

\section{Phased mode}

When performing time sensitive tasks or entering contests or combat, the game enters phased mode.

The GM rotates through different phases where one party is on offense and the other parties are on defense. The GM chooses which party starts on offense based on who initiated the combat.

Defenders choose their reactions first during a turn, and then offense chooses their actions. If one particular character initiated combat with action of any kind, that action occurs before the defenders choose their reactions.

The players within a party may choose their actions and reactions in any order, and do not need to wait until one player has completed all possible actions before performing their own.

\subsection{The Arena}
When the game enters phased mode the GM must specify an arena. The arena rules are designed to be able to quickly generate unique scenarios.

An arena consists of 1 or more rooms.

Each room contains
\begin{itemize}
	\item 1 or more spaces
	\item 0 or more cover spots
	\item 1 or more connections to other rooms
	\item 0 or more exits from the arena
\end{itemize}

Characters have tokens or coins that represent them that can be moved from room to room, or placed on a cover spot, to indicate where they are physically.

A room can be entered even if it is at capacity, but at the end of each phase, if there are more characters than spaces in the room, the GM chooses characters that are not in covered spots to be pushed out of the room by the action until there are only as many characters in the room as there are spaces.

Characters in covered spots may remain in a room even if the room is over capacity.

It takes a movement action to get into or out of a cover spot. A cover spot can only be entered if it is unoccupied.
Characters not in a cover spot move freely within the room without a move action.

A character behind cover has any incoming damage reduced by the amount of cover.

Connections are drawn between rooms with a double line. A connection which leads to open space rather than another room indicates an exit from the arena.

Traps outside a connector act like cover spaces- they require a movement to step into, and on moving into a trap, they deal the amount of damage indicated. The type of damage dealt depends on the type of trap. The GM may write P, E, or D next to a trap to indicate that it deals physical, elemental, or direct damage respectively.

A character encountering a trap must roll \GRPtalent{Dexterity}. The damage a trap deals is reduced by the number of successes on the Dexterity roll.

Armor protects against trap damage unless the trap deals direct damage. Characters may also Parry physical or elemental damage from a trap.

Note that if your players are creative with their surroundings, you may end up adding, changing, or removing rooms, connections, hazards, and traps as the turn-based gameplay progresses!

The fastest way to generate an arena is collaboratively-- when an arena is needed, the GM should describe the rooms and connectors, and the players should help draw what the GM is describing.

\subsection{Ranges}

Every action a character can perform has a range.

\begin{GRPquicknote}

{\large Single Target Ranges}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Self}-- Only affects the character performing the action
	\item \textbf{Touch}-- Affects any one target in the same room as the character
	\item \textbf{Reach}-- Affects any one target within the same room as the character, or any adjacent rooms connected directly to the current room.
	\item \textbf{Line-of-sight}-- Affects any one target the character has line-of-sight to.
	\item \textbf{Awareness}-- Affects any one target the character can presently detect via any of their senses.
\end{itemize}

{\large Area of Effect Ranges}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Room}-- Affects all objects and characters within the room.
	\item \textbf{Connected Rooms}-- Affects all objects and characters in this room and all connected rooms
	\item \textbf{Map}-- Affects all objects and characters within the current map.
	\item \textbf{Line-of-sight Room}-- Affects all objects and characters in any one room the character has line of sight to.
\end{itemize}
\end{GRPquicknote}

Ranges are particularly relevant in phased mode, but may be applicable in other scenarios as well.



\subsection{Offense}

Each character can take 3 offensive actions during each offensive phase.

They may choose freely from any offensive actions available to them and perform one of the options multiple times if they want.

Your possible actions are:

\begin{itemize}
	\item Interact
	\item Apply talent
	\item Move
	\item Attack
	\item Obstruct
	\item Use other ability or item that is labeled ``as an action'' or ``as an offensive action''
\end{itemize}

A description of each of these options follows:

\begin{GRPquicknotewide}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Interact} \emph{Touch range}-- Flip a switch, pull a lever, pick up an object, drop an object, or perform any other interaction that does not require a talent roll.
	\item \textbf{Apply Talent} \emph{Touch range}-- Directly apply one of the character's talents to any target within the room.
	\item \textbf{Move} \emph{Self range}-- Move this character. Typically one move action is required to move from one room to another, but depending on the arena, additional movement may be needed.
	\item \textbf{Attack}-- Attack, either using a tool/weapon, or your body.

	When taking the attack action, you may make as many attacks as it takes to use all your \GRPstat{Grab Limbs} (the number of prehensile limbs your character has). Some attacks may use up more than one of your limbs to use. Your options are:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item \textbf{Weapon Attack}-- for each attack action, a character may only attack once with each weapon they have currently equipped. Weapons have an associated talent to use them, and weapons have a minimum or maximum number of limbs you may use to attack.

			A weapon attack roll is involves rolling the associated talent, with bonus dice equal to the power of the weapon, and additional advantage equal to the number of limbs used to attack.

			The number of successes on your roll indicates the amount of damage you do, and the type of damage is dependent on the weapon.

			\item \textbf{Barehanded Attack} \emph{Touch range}-- Choose a number of limbs you are going to make a single attack with. Roll \GRPtalent{Fortitude}\GRPaa{\GRPstat{Limbs}}.

			The number of successes on your roll indicates the amount of physical damage you do.

			\item \textbf{Grapple} \emph{Touch range}-- Involves any maneuver meant to move another character against their will. This involves a roll contest where a character rolls \GRPtalent{Athletics} and the target contests with \GRPtalent{Fortitude}. A character within cover cannot initiate a grapple, but a character outside cover may initiate a grapple with a character within cover.

			If you win the contest, you may force the target to make a single move action of your choice.

			Additional forced movement requires additional grapple actions.

			\item \textbf{Disable} \emph{Touch range}-- Impair another character from accessing their augments for the rest of phased mode. This could involve attacking a pressure point or critical point meant to prevent the flow of energy to a character's augments.

			The initiating character rolls \GRPtalent{Dexterity} and the target contests with \GRPtalent{Fortitude}. 

			If you win the contest, the target's \GRPstat{Max Augments} is temporarily reduced by 1 and they must choose augments to temporarily disable until they are below their new \GRPstat{Max Augments}. Their \GRPstat{Max Augments} is restored to normal as soon as Phased mode ends.
		\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Obstruct} \emph{self range}-- The character blocks one connector attached to the room they are in. They must be forced to make a Move action or be rendered incapacitated before anyone else can travel through that connector. This is generally indicated by placing your character token on top of the connector on the side of the room. 
\end{itemize}
\end{GRPquicknotewide}

\subsection{Defense}

When you are defending, each character in the defending party must choose one reaction.

Your possible reactions are:

\begin{itemize}
	\item Protect
	\item Retaliate
	\item Obstruct
	\item Dodge
	\item Use other ability or item that is labeled "as a defensive reaction"
\end{itemize}

For each of these, you must also specify your reaction trigger. Your choices of trigger are:

\begin{itemize}
	\item the start of the phase
	\item an action taken targeting another character
	\item an action taken targeting your character
	\item an action taken by another character
	\item a character entering a room
	\item a character exiting a room
\end{itemize}

A description of each of the options follows:

\begin{GRPquicknotewide}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Protect}-- chose any one character or object within the same zone as this character. Once triggered, the first time an action is taken against the character or object being protected, the action is made to target the protecting character instead of its initial target. You can indicate what is being protected by placing the protecting character's token half way over the target. If multiple characters take the protect reaction on a single target, they may choose between themselves in what order they protect when triggered.
	\item \textbf{Retaliate}-- you may take one non-movement offensive action (as in the offense phase) in response to the trigger.
	\item \textbf{Obstruct}-- In response to the trigger, you block one connector. You must be forced to make a Move action or be rendered incapacitated before anyone else can travel through that connector. This is generally indicated by placing your character token on top of the connector on the side of the room.
	\item \textbf{Dodge}-- you may take one movement action in response to the trigger.
\end{itemize}
\end{GRPquicknotewide}

On a tabletop- you have the choice of sharing your defensive action with the GM or of writing it on a card and turning it face down
