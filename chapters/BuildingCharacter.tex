\GRPchapter{Building a Character}

The same character building process is used for non-player-characters (NPC's)\index{NPC} and player characters (PC's)\index{PC}. This chapter will indicate when something is nonessential for a non-player character.

For a step by step overview of the process of building a character, consider skipping to Section \ref{sec:character_building_overview}.
\vspace{3\baselineskip}

\section{Fundamental Attributes}

All characters have 6 fundamental attributes, divided into 3 \emph{Foundations} and 3 \emph{Characteristics}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Foundations}\index{Foundation}-- \\ Mind, Body, Heart
\item \textbf{Characteristics}\index{Characteristic}-- \\ Precision, Strength, Grace
\end{itemize}

The foundations and characteristics are combined to form 9 fundamental \emph{talents}\index{Talent} that all characters have.

\noindent When building a character, you have:

\begin{itemize}
\item 6 points to distribute among your foundations (\GRPstat{Mind}, \GRPstat{Body}, \GRPstat{Heart}) as you see fit
\item 8 points to distribute among your characteristics (\GRPstat{Precision}, \GRPstat{Strength}, \GRPstat{Grace}) as you see fit
\end{itemize}

You \textbf{must} allocate at least 1 point to each foundation and each characteristic.

The number of points you allocate to each fundamental attribute become the value of that fundamental attribute for the lifetime of your character and may not be changed after character creation.

However, you can gain aptitude in each of the fundamental talents which give additional advantage to their rolls, and racial bonuses and skills may give you bonus dice on different rolls.

The 12 fundamental talents are meant to intuitively follow from their construction. If you are trying to determine what talents are applicable in a situation, simply consider:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Does this situation need Precision, Strength, or Grace?
	\item Does this situation relate to the Body, the Heart, or the Mind?
\end{itemize}
Then, find the corresponding row and column on the talent grid, and roll that talent to determine success.

The GM ultimately determines if a talent applies in a narrative situation, and sometimes there may be more than one talent that is equally applicable. If the player can make an argument that the GM finds reasonable for why a talent is reasonable in a situation, they may roll that talent.

For clarity, what follows are descriptions of all the talents along with some example situations where they apply. This is not a comprehensive list.

\begin{GRPquicknotewide}
\index{Talent!Examples}%
\begin{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Memory}-- Used for any situation that requires Precision of Mind, such as precise recall, exact pattern matching or recognition, or accurate calculation. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item A character is trying to recall if they've seen a symbol before in their past
			\item A character is trying to determine which of two piles of material weighs more
			\item A character is trying to determine if a language or code they are hearing is one they've heard before.
			\item A character is trying to memorize instructions for a complex process
		\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Intellect}-- Used for any situation that requires Strength of Mind-- such as intense computation or decisions involving many simultaneous variables. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item A character is trying to decode a coded message
			\item A character is coming up with the solution to a puzzle
			\item A character needs to beat another character in a game of chess
		\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Insight}-- Used for any situation that requires Grace of Mind, such as nimble thinking or problems requiring the right inspiration to strike in a timely manner. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item A character is investigating a crime scene
			\item A character is trying to invent a mechanism to close a door based on objects strewn around the area
			\item A character is trying to discern the political motivations of an organization
			\item A character is trying to escape a trap or legal dilemma
		\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Dexterity}-- Used for any situation that requires Precision of Body-- think along the lines of quick and accurate reflexes. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Aiming a ranged weapon at a moving target
			\tcbbreak
			\item Catching a rapidly moving small object
			\item Disarming a delicate trap without triggering it
		\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Fortitude}-- Used for any situation that requires Strength of Body-- think along the lines of endurance and raw physical power. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Withstanding the effects of a strong alcoholic drink
			\item Carrying a heavy object for a period of time
			\item Wading through thick mud or pushing through tangled vines
		\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Athletics}-- Used for any situation that requires Grace of Body-- think along the lines of well developed martial form or postural technique. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Throwing an object as far as possible
			\item Arm wrestling another character
			\item Rolling out of a fall to mitigate damage
		\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Perception}-- Used for any situation that requires Precision of Heart. This applies to the accuracy of a character's emotional inspiration. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Determining if another character is lying
			\item Accurately guessing why another character is acting strange
			\item Figuring out what is meaningful to another character
		\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Will}-- Used for any situation that requires Strength of Heart. This applies to determination and emotional centered-ness. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item A character is trying to withstand a psychic attack (or puppy-dog eyes)
			\item A character is trying to power through pain to maintain focus on a task
			\item A character is trying to resist the effects of a sedative
		\end{itemize}
	\item \GRPtalent{Charisma}-- Used for any situation that requires Grace of Heart. This can be thought of the character's ability to navigate emotional issues and convince other people to follow their lead. Examples include:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Persuading another character to give up an item
			\item Rallying a crowd to perform an action
			\item Deceiving another character about something
		\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\end{GRPquicknotewide}

\subsection{Talent Proxying}

The various talents in \GammaRP{} have significant conceptual overlap. In addition to the GM determining what talents are appropriate to a situation, players are allowed to use talents on the opposite side of the talent grid at 2 additional disadvantage. This is called talent proxying.

This encapsulates the fact that vastly different skills may be used to accomplish the same feat. For instance, the ability to remember details about what motivates people can substitute for the ability to appeal to their emotions, and in this way, the \GRPtalent{Memory} talent may proxy for the \GRPtalent{Charisma} talent. Fast enough reflexes would allow you to adjust your body in the right way to accomplish martial feats that would usually require well developed form. Conversely, well developed form can substitute for fast and accurate reflexes. Therefore \GRPtalent{Dexterity} and \GRPtalent{Athletics} can proxy for each other.

\GRPtalent{Fortitude} may be proxied with either \GRPtalent{Intellect}, \GRPtalent{Dexterity}, \GRPtalent{Will}, or \GRPtalent{Athletics} at 4 additional disadvantage.

This represents the fact that physical problem solving (Intellect), accurate enough reflexes (Dexterity), dogged enough determination (Will), or well developed form (Athletics), can all substitute for raw power.

You can gain aptitude in your talents by spending experience points, or XP for short. Each aptitude gives 1 additional advantage when rolling the talent.

It costs:
\begin{itemize}
	\item 1 XP for the first aptitude
	\item 2 XP for the second aptitude
	\item 3 XP for the third aptitude
\end{itemize}

You may have a maximum of 3 aptitude in any talent.

\subsection{Depleting Fundamentals}

In a pinch, you may \emph{deplete} your foundations and your characteristics for certain bonuses.

Each time you \emph{deplete} a fundamental, you incur an additional -1 penalty to that fundamental. This penalty persists until you next take the \emph{sleep} downtime action. You may not deplete your foundations or characteristics below 0.

\vspace{\baselineskip}
\begin{GRPquicknote}
Note that if you deplete your \GRPstat{Mind}, \GRPstat{Body}, or \GRPstat{Heart} to 0 you will \textbf{automatically fail all rolls related to these foundations}. You may still proxy a related talent to avoid failing the roll.
\end{GRPquicknote}

Depleting 2 foundations (\GRPstat{Mind}, \GRPstat{Body}, or \GRPstat{Heart}) to 0 causes a character to fall \emph{unconscious}. Your character may return to consciousness by having one of their foundations restored.

Depleting all 3 foundations (\GRPstat{Mind}, \GRPstat{Body}, and \GRPstat{Heart}) to 0, \emph{kills} a character. If your character is dead- talents, abilities and items that normally restore foundation depletion will not work, and they must be restored to life by other means.

Any or all of a character's characteristics (\GRPstat{Precision}, \GRPstat{Strength}, or \GRPstat{Grace}) may be depleted to 0 without penalty.

\section{Wounds}

Each time a character receives a wound, they must choose a foundation (\GRPstat{Mind}, \GRPstat{Body}, or \GRPstat{Heart}) to deplete by 1.

Healing wounds in this system is done by abilities that restore depleted foundations.

The \textbf{Robust} skill (described in Section \ref{sec:skills}) allows a character to take some wounds before depleting foundations.

\subsection{Injuries}

An injury is a specific type of wound which is applied directly to one specific foundation (one of \GRPstat{Mind}, \GRPstat{Body}, or \GRPstat{Heart}).

The GM decides if a task has a risk of injury. Injuries are incurred specifically to the foundation related to the talent used for the failed task.

Failed \GRPtalent{Memory}, \GRPtalent{Intellect}, and \GRPtalent{Insight} rolls with a risk of injury result in an injury to \GRPstat{Mind}.

Failed \GRPtalent{Dexterity}, \GRPtalent{Fortitude}, and \GRPtalent{Athletics} rolls with a risk of injury result in an injury to \GRPstat{Body}.

Failed \GRPtalent{Perception}, \GRPtalent{Will}, and \GRPtalent{Charisma} rolls with a risk of injury result in an injury to \GRPstat{Heart}.

The Robust skill still protects you from injuries if you have wounds left that can be allocated to the Robust skill.

\section{Species Bonuses}

The species of a character provides bonuses to some of their talents.

The species bonuses are described for each species in Chapter \ref{chap:species}.

\section{Archetypes}

Your character has an archetype-- a way that their luck tends to run. This consists of a general type of \emph{situation} and the type of \emph{fortune} they have in this situation.

Your character starts with 1 archetype rank, and additional ranks may be added for 3 XP each. You may have a maximum of 5 archetype ranks.

You may choose to accumulate bonus archetype ranks above your usual maximum by \emph{depleting} 1 from Grace for each bonus rank.

\begin{GRPquicknotewide}
\noindent{\large\bfseries Situations:}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Social}-- all circumstances in which the character is trying to influence the actions or words of other characters in the world around them. Recieve +1 to \GRPstat{Grace}. 
	\item \textbf{Technical}-- all circumstances in which the character is trying to create or modify something in the world around them. Recieve +1 to \GRPstat{Precision}. 
	\item \textbf{Martial}-- all circumstances in which the character is in combat or physical contest (with the environment or another player). Recieve +1 to \GRPstat{Strength}.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{4\baselineskip}
\noindent{\large\bfseries Fortunes:}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Adept}-- For any roll in your situation, you may adjust any die by up to \GRPstat{Ranks} values AFTER rolling.
	\item \textbf{Awkward}-- Up to \GRPstat{Ranks} times a day, you may choose to automatically fail a roll in your situation in order to gain 4 bonus dice on your next roll (in any situation).
	\item \textbf{Confident}-- Once a day, after making a successful roll in a situation, you may choose to gain 6 advantage for the next \GRPstat{Ranks} consecutive subsequent rolls in that situation. You must do this before seeing the results of any of the subsequent rolls.
	\item \textbf{Duplicitious}-- When your character is in a contested roll in the situation, you may swap up to \GRPstat{Ranks} of your rolled dice with your opponent's rolled dice (even if your opponent is inanimate or the environment). Your number of successes still depend on how much advantage you had on your original roll, and your opponent's number of successes still depend on how much advantage they had in their original roll.
	\item \textbf{Judicious}--  Roll \GRPstat{Ranks} dice at the beginning of the day. You maintain a pool of \GRPstat{Ranks} dice that starts initially populated by your first rolls. In any archetype situation- you may swap any number of dice in your pool with dice in your roll, AFTER seeing the result.
	\item \textbf{Lucky}-- A number of times equal to your number of archetype ranks, you may choose to automatically succeed on a roll in this situation. You must choose to do this before seeing the result of the roll.
	\item \textbf{Oblivious}-- Up to \GRPstat{Ranks} times a day in your character's situation, you may ignore a roll and force a reroll, AFTER seeing the result, and you may reverse decisions about whether you used another ability to alter the dice roll before rerolling it.
\end{itemize}
\end{GRPquicknotewide}

\section{Loadout Overview}

A character's \emph{loadout} refers to the set of equipment and abilities that they currently have access to. In \GammaRP{} these are split into three categories: Casts, Augments, and Equipment.

Since there are numerous options for each of the loadout categories, this section will simply describe the function of each loadout category without listing all the options. For more details on various loadout options, skip to Chapter \ref{chap:loadout_options}.

For NPC's a GM may consider skipping fully defining a loadout. In most situations an NPC will function even if the loadout has sections that are not defined.

\subsection{Casts}

\emph{Casts} are special abilities that may be used a limited number of times each day. .

A character may only have a fixed number of \emph{prepared casts}. On creation, a character's \GRPstat{Max Prep} is equal to their \GRPstat{Mind} score. They may prep more casts than this by depleting their \GRPstat{Mind} score by 1 for each additional cast prepared. The \GRPstat{Mind} score depleted by overprepping casts may be restored by unprepping these casts.

In order to prepare a cast, a character must possess a cast focus corresponding to that cast. Most cast focuses are Wt 0 and cost 100 gold. Once a cast focus is in a character's posession it may be used as many times as desired to prepare that cast. A cast focus may be spell notes that a character has written themselves, or a series of runes that they use to imbue themselves with power, or even a device they activate which creates an effect on demand.

A character may only activate any of their prepared casts a fixed number of times between downtimes. On creation, a character's \GRPstat{Max Casts} is equal to their \GRPstat{Precision}. 

To be clear, all of these cast activations may be due to the same cast being used, but a character may only cast a number of times equal to \GRPstat{Max Casts} without penalty. They may cast more than their usual max by depleting their \GRPstat{Precision} by 1 for each time they exceed \GRPstat{Max Casts}.

Options for casts in the base system are specified in Section \ref{sec:casts}.

\subsection{Augments}

\emph{Augments} are devices, implants, or enhanced body features that grant a character extra abilities.

A character's \GRPstat{Max Augments}, the number of augments they may have active without penalty, is initially equal to their \GRPstat{Heart} score. They may have more active augments than their \GRPstat{Max Augments} by depleting their \GRPstat{Heart} score for one by every additional augment they keep active. The \GRPstat{Heart} score depleted by overactivating augments can be restored by deactivating augments over the limit.

Each augment in general gives a character access to one ability.

The exception is \emph{attunements} which are special augments that give a character access to a skill tree that can be invested in with XP. Each attunement counts as \textbf{two} augments. Attunements can be very powerful but can be riskier- if a character is \emph{disabled} and has to deactivate an attunement, they will lose access to the entire skill tree provided by an augment.

Options for augments in the base system are specified in Section \ref{sec:augments}

\subsection{Equipment}

On creation, a character's \GRPstat{Equip Wt}, the total weight of equipment they can have actively equipped is equal to their \GRPstat{Body} score. If a character equips equipment in excess of the \GRPstat{Equip Wt}, they will \emph{deplete} their \GRPstat{Body} score by 1 for each additional weight unit of equipment they equip. The \GRPstat{Body} score depleted by overqeuipping equipment may be restored by unequipping the additional equipment.

Equipment is divided into armor and implements. Implements include weapons, tools, and accessories, as well as any other items that may need to be rapidly accessed, manipulated, or wielded during \emph{phased} mode. A character may only have one piece of armor equipment actively equipped. A character may have any number of implweapons, tools and accessories actively equipped as long as the sum of their weights does not exceed the character's \GRPstat{Equip Wt}.

Only actively equipped items may be accessed without penalty when the game is in \emph{phased} mode.

On creation, a character's \GRPstat{Pack Wt}, total weight of things they can have in their personal inventory (aside from equipment you currently have actively equipped) is equal to their \GRPstat{Strength} score. Having more weight than this in their inventory will \emph{deplete} their \GRPstat{Strength} score by 1 for each additional weight unit of items they have in their inventory. The \GRPstat{Strength} score depleted by carrying too many items may be restored by dropping items in their inventory.

Options for equipment in the base system are specified in Section \ref{sec:equipment}.

\subsection{Changing Loadout}

If you change your loadout of active augments, inventory items, or prepped casts during \emph{phased} (turn-based) gameplay, you will \emph{deplete} your \GRPstat{Grace} score. You may change your actively equipped items without penalty during phased gameplay, but note that pulling items out of your pack to do so will still deplete your \GRPstat{Grace}.

You can change any part of your loadout without penalty during \emph{open} (non-turn-based) gameplay however many times you like.

\section{Languages}

Languages may be modified to be setting specific in \GammaRP{}, but there are 6 languages that are referred to in the base system listed in this section.

A character starts knowing Standard, and depending on your character's species may know one other language by default. Your character may learn a new language by investing 2XP.

\begin{GRPquicknotewide}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Standard}-- A common language spoken by most sentient creatures. A cultural relic of a vast fallen empire which used to rule the setting.
	\item \textbf{Leesong}-- A language that is part of the cultural heritage of the Avian species.
	\item \textbf{Chitter}-- A language that is part of the cultural heritage of the Weaver Species.
	\item \textbf{Wake}-- A language that is part of the cultural heritage of the Aquatic species.
	\item \textbf{Slewbask}-- A language that is part of the cultural heritage of the Reptilian species.
	\item \textbf{Prineu}--A language that is part of the cultural heritage of the Homonid species.
\end{itemize}
\end{GRPquicknotewide}

The GM may add languages and dialects beyond these to their setting as they see fit

\section{Skills}\label{sec:skills}

Skills are things your character has trained in which confer various benefits. You must invest in skills with XP in order to use them. 

\noindent It costs:
\begin{itemize}
	\item 1 XP for the first rank in a skill
	\item 2 XP for the second rank in a skill
	\item 3 XP for the third rank in a skill.
\end{itemize}

Each skill may only be listed once (with the exception of the ``Experienced'' skill), and you may have a maximum of 3 ranks in any skill.

What follows are descriptions of all the skills your character can take.

\begin{GRPquicknotewide}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Augment Mastery}-- for each rank in augment mastery, you may increase your \GRPstat{Max Augments} by one.
	\item \textbf{Marathon Caster}-- for each rank in marathon caster, you may increase your \GRPstat{Max Cast Activations} by one
	\item \textbf{Versatile Caster}-- for each rank in versatile caster, you may increase your \GRPstat{Max Prepared Casts} by one.
	\item \textbf{Robust}-- you may take wounds up to \GRPstat{Ranks} in Robust without depleting any of your fundamentals. Once you exceed this number of wounds you will start depleting fundamentals as usual in response to sustaining a wound.
	\item \textbf{Porter}-- for each rank in porter, you may increase your \GRPstat{Pack Wt} by 1.
	\item \textbf{Walking Armory}-- for each rank in Walking Armory, you may increase your \GRPstat{Equip Wt} by 1.
	\item \textbf{Linguist}-- Whenever you must roll for any sort of communication involving a language your character does not know, you may do so with \GRPstat{Ranks} additional advantage.
	\item \textbf{Experienced in [topic]}-- pick a topic your character is experienced in. The topic must be much more specific than "social," "martial," or "technical." Whenever your character is rolling in a situation relating to this topic, gain \GRPstat{ranks} bonus dice to your roll. You may take this skill multiple times as long as the topic is unique for each listing.

	Examples of topics for Experienced:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Unarmed combat
		\item A specific class of spaceships
		\item A specific type of magic
		\item A specific type of weapon
		\item A specific culture
		\item A specific technology
	\end{itemize}

	The GM of a game can decide if the topic your character has picked is specific enough for the Experienced skill. If a topic you've already chosen seems too broad after a few sessions of play, the GM can ask you to narrow your topic. If a topic you've chosen seems too narrow after a few sessions of play, you may ask the GM to broaden your topic.
\end{itemize}
\end{GRPquicknotewide}

\section{Character Building Overview}\label{sec:character_building_overview}

For convenience, this section is a consolidated step-by-step guide to building a character. You may follow along with the example character sheet at the end of this section.

\begin{enumerate}
	\item Chose your Fundamentals. Your foundations (\GRPstat{Heart}, \GRPstat{Body}, and \GRPstat{Mind}) should all sum up to 6. Your characteristics (\GRPstat{Precision}, \GRPstat{Strength}, \GRPstat{Grace}) should all sum up to 8. The minimum value of any of these fundamentals is 1.
	\item Choose a species. Apply any bonuses to talents by writing them in the corresponding box labeled with \GRPdicebonus{}. Take note of any species abilities.
	\item Write ``Standard'' in your languages. If your species gives you another language by default, write this in your languages as well.
	\item Set your \GRPstat{Max Casts} to your \GRPstat{Precision} score, and set your \GRPstat{Max Prep} to your \GRPstat{Mind} score.
	\item Set your \GRPstat{Equip Wt} to your \GRPstat{Body} score, and set your \GRPstat{Pack Wt} to your \GRPstat{Strength} score.
	\item Set your \GRPstat{Max Augments} to your \GRPstat{Heart} score.

	\item Start with 12 XP. Invest as you see fit in various abilities, or save them for later.
	\item Start with 100 gold. Invest in equipment as you see fit, or save it for later.
	\item Choose a number of casts less than or equal to your \GRPstat{Max Prep} and write them in your list of prepped casts. Place corresponding cast focuses in the cast focus section of your Pack.
	\item Choose a number of augments less than or equal to your \GRPstat{Max Augments} and write them in your list of augments.
	\item If you have taken the robust skill, you can put the number of skill ranks you have in robust in the smaller box of the ``Wounds'' section of the character sheet.
	\item Discard items of your choice in your pack until the total weight of pack items is less than or equal to \GRPstat{Pack Wt}.
\end{enumerate}
