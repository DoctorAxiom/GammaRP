\GRPchapter{The Basics}

\GammaRP{} is a tabletop role-playing game. This is a specific class of improvisational acting game in which the participants are divided into \emph{Game Masters} (often referred to as GM's) and \emph{Players}. Most games will require at least one game master in order to play the game, and the game master and players work together to tell a story.

The \index{Game Master}Game Master is responsible for constructing the world around the Players, and giving the players a setting to interact with. The \index{Player}Players are responsible for maintaining a Player Character-- a character who interacts with and influences the setting defined by the Game Master. 

Since the game is collaborative in this way, it is more fun and fair if the Game Master does not know the outcome of all actions in the setting. In order to fairly decide events where the outcome is unsure-- \GammaRP{} uses the roll of a set of dice to decide whether a course of action is successful.

\section{The Fundamental Dice Mechanic}

The fundamental dice mechanic in \GammaRP{} is based on 12 sided dice. If you intend to play the game on a real physical tabletop, you may want to obtain a few dozen 12 sided dice.

Typically, the notation for a roll is written as \GRProlla{X}{Y}

This means that you roll \textit{X} 12 sided dice. If you recieve a 12 on any dice, the roll is a success.

\textit{Y} represents the \emph{advantage} you receive on the roll. Higher numbers mean the roll is successful even at lower thresholds. An advantage of 1 is the default when advantage is not specified. This means that the roll as normal is only successful on a 12. However an advantage of 2 means the roll is successful on any instance of a 12 or an 11 (there are two numbers now on which the roll is successful). In general an advantage of \textit{Y} means the top \textit{Y} numbers on the dice will count as a success. An advantage of 0 is the exception, which still is successful if any dice rolls a 12.

An advantage of 12 means that the roll automatically succeeds.

\emph{Advantage} can also be negative. For convenience this is written as \emph{disadvantage} which is the negative of the advantage. A roll of \GRProlla{X}{(-Z)} is written equivalently as \GRProlld{X}{Z}.

A roll with at \emph{disadvantage} of \textit{Z} means that \textit{Z} dice within the roll need to land on 12 for the roll to be successful. For example, with a roll of \GRProll{4}{-2}, 2 of the 4 dice need to land on 12 in order for the roll to be successful.

\begin{GRPquicknote}
Note that the rolls \GRProll{X}{1}, \GRProll{X}{0}, \GRProll{X}{-1} are all identical, requiring a single dice of the set to result 12 in order to result a success.
\end{GRPquicknote}

When the disadvantage is greater than the number of dice being rolled, the roll is an automatic failure, since there is no way to obtain a number of successful rolls higher than the number of dice being rolled.

With more than 2 disadvantage on any roll, the probability of a successful roll rapidly diminishes. A disadvantage of 12 or greater is considered an automatic failure for the roll (even though there is theoretically a chance of 12 twelve-sided dice all rolling 12). This is meant to simply be symmetric with the advantage mechanic where an advantage of 12 guarantees success. 

In general, when a character has more than 2 disadvantage on a roll they will not succeed unless they use a dice modifying ability.
